# What a package may provide and obsolete, as an extended regular expression per package
# kind, matched against the whole of a relation's name (the version after it is not read).
# A release that signs a package claiming to obsolete something else, such as `openssh`, is
# refused: the signature says who made a package, not that its claims are the product's own.
# Conflicts and Obsoletes of a `static` or `extra` package are not allowed at all, and nothing is
# allowed to supplement or enhance: those two relations attach a package to other packages from the
# outside. `requires`, `recommends` and `suggests` say what a package may pull in; NONE means nothing.
#
# A `service` is a `static` product that installs a systemd unit: the same rules, except that it also says in
# service-requires.txt, product by product, the exact Requires it may add (name, comparison and version), and carries the
# three scriptlets of the systemd macros (scriptlets.txt).
#
# The fourth column is the form of the comparison and the version that go with a name: NONE (neither), PROVIDED (none, or
# `=` and a version) and LOWERBOUND (none, or `>=`, `=` or `>` and a version; never `<` or `<=`, which force a host to an old
# version). The Podman kinds name no version for what they obsolete: it is unversioned in the contract.
#
